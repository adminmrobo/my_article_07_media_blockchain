1Question

How few hands hold the tokens?

Paper: Section 4.2 · Figures 6·7Ledger: who holds the vault keys?

A problem often debated in the token economy is decentralization. The original holds that token creation, distribution and the resources for making tokens should not be concentrated on one side, and proposes measuring decentralization by each account's token holding share, borrowing the Gini coefficient and the decile ratio that economics uses to measure income inequality. On this page we also open, for the first time, the holding concentration data for 91 ERC-20 tokens that the author collected separately.

Common belief

Many accounts means it is decentralized. As users grow over time, tokens spread out on their own.

What the original measures

Not the number of accounts, but which floor the holdings are piled on. In the example of original Figure 7, the top three levels (450 accounts) hold 81.5% of all tokens (K% Rule indicator).

3Result 1

Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient — line up and add up

Model: Gini = A ÷ (A + B)Ledger: sharing gold coins among ten people

Seen as a ledger

Suppose you share 100 gold coins among ten people. Line them up from the one who got the least and draw the cumulative coins: an equal split gives the diagonal, and one person taking all gives a bent line stuck to the bottom.

In the paper

Figure 6 left: a Lorenz curve drawn with the cumulative share of population (horizontal) and the cumulative share of income (vertical). The larger the area (A) between the line of perfect equality and the Lorenz curve, the greater the inequality. Right: income share of the bottom 40% of households ÷ income share of the top 20% (decile ratio).

Sharing gold coins among ten people — change them by hand

Example

Explanation — reading two numbers

  1. Gini coefficient: 0 means everyone has the same; near 1 means one person has almost everything. It is A — the area of the triangle under the diagonal (0.5) minus the area under the Lorenz curve (B) — divided by 0.5.
  2. Decile ratio: share of the bottom 40% ÷ share of the top 20%. The larger, the more equal (with perfect equality, 40 ÷ 20 = 2).

Limit of this box: ten people is easy, but token accounts number in the hundreds of thousands, and mostly we only know “totals by range”. That is why the original groups accounts into levels → next box.

the Doctor

The Doctor's one line: the Gini coefficient tells “how much” it is concentrated; the decile ratio tells “how different the two ends are”.

4Result 2

Original Figure 7 — nine levels and the K% Rule indicator

Model: sum of holding shares of the top k levelsLedger: the vault's floor-by-floor ledger

Seen as a ledger

This table sorts the vault ledger by “balance range”. Floor 1 has many people but few gold coins; the top floor has only ten people but a pile of gold coins.

In the paper

Divide accounts into levels 1~9 by holdings, and for each level write the number of accounts (A), total tokens held (B), mean per account (C = B/A) and holding share (D). The share sum of top levels 7~9, 81.5%, is shown as the K% Rule indicator.

Seeing it in action — the account pyramid and the gold-coin pyramid

Explanation — what we computed from the table

  1. K% Rule indicator: shares of the top 3 levels (400 + 40 + 10 = 450 accounts) 38.8% + 11.4% + 31.3% = 81.5% (original).
  2. Level Gini coefficient: a value computed by lining up each level as one block, drawing a Lorenz curve (computed on this page; it assumes everyone in a level is equal, so it comes out smaller than reality).
  3. The Gini coefficient changes depending on whether empty accounts (level 1) are included or excluded. That is why “many accounts” does not mean decentralization.

Caution when reading: some rows in the original table have means (C) that do not fit the level range (level 1 is “0 tokens” but the mean is 1.0, level 2 is “1~9 tokens” but the mean is 375.4, and levels 4·5 have means 21.5·18.6). The calculation C = B/A is correct, so it is safest to read this table as example figures that show the method.

5Result 3

Real data — concentration K1~K4 of 91 ERC-20 tokens (2016.4~2018.11)

Data: author's Excel DATA_DISTRIBUTION_ALL_COINLedger: logbooks of 91 vaults

Seen as a ledger

We took one line per week from the logbooks of 91 vaults and drew “what share of the gold coins the top tiers hold”.

In the data

Each token has daily concentration indicators K1~K4 and a holding size K5. K1~K4 express the cumulative holding share of top tiers on a 0–1 scale like a Gini coefficient; the closer to 1, the more it is concentrated in a few accounts. The four indicators differ in where they cut the top tiers (K1 is the largest).

Explanation — what we did when handling this data

  1. K1~K4 values at or below 0 or above 1 (e.g. 0, 2, 5, 10) are outside the indicator range, so they were removed from charts and statistics.

6Lab

Model lab — what reduces concentration?

Model: Gini coefficient of 1,000 accounts · top 1% shareLedger: handing out vault keys

Seen as a ledger

Even if many new customers sign up in the vault ledger, if they buy no gold coins the ledger just gets longer. Only when whales let go of their gold coins do the coins spread out.

In the model

Start with 1,000 accounts whose holdings follow a concentrated distribution (Pareto). Each month, ① new empty accounts appear, ② new accounts buy tokens in the market (as much as whales sold), or ③ nothing happens. We track the Gini coefficient and the top 1% share.

Concentration control panel — change only one thing at a time

New empty accounts per month
Whale selling rate (monthly)
Empty account

Explanation

  1. If only empty accounts grow, the Gini coefficient actually goes up (if you count them). Account count is not evidence of decentralization.
  2. The top 1% share falls only when tokens sold by whales spread to new accounts.
  3. So the original proposes measuring by each level's holding share, not by the number of accounts.

Limit: the Pareto distribution, the selling rate and the rule that new accounts buy equal amounts are all assumptions. In real tokens, one exchange wallet holds the tokens of many users, so concentration by address can come out higher than concentration by real person.

7Reread

Reread — in the language of the ledger

Lorenz curveLedger: gold coins added up from those who hold the least

The farther from the diagonal, the more concentrated. Limit: the picture changes with the unit you line up (people, accounts, wallets).

Gini coefficientLedger: concentration as one number from 0 to 1

Easy to compare. Limit: one number does not show where it is concentrated (at the top or the bottom).

Decile ratioLedger: bottom 40% ÷ top 20%

Compares the two ends directly. Limit: it ignores the middle 60–80% range.

K% RuleLedger: the share held by the top few floors

Shows whale concentration directly. Limit: the value changes depending on what k you choose.

Empty accountLedger: a customer with only a name

Inflates the account count. Limit: they may be future buyers.

Exchange walletLedger: a big vault holding many customers' gold coins

Inflates concentration. Limit: you cannot know the owner from the address alone.

One-line formula: decentralization is not the number of accounts but the distribution of holding shares · Gini = A ÷ (A+B) · K% = Σ share of top k levels · real data: K1 median –, no clear decrease over two years

In generative AI terms

The Gini coefficient is also used in AI to measure “concentration”. For example, we draw the same Lorenz curve to check whether training data is piled on a few sources or a few languages, or whether a recommender system keeps showing only a few popular products (popularity bias).

Limit of the analogy: tokens can be moved by buying and selling, but data concentration changes only by collecting new data.

In the field

When evaluating token distribution, ① mark empty accounts and exchange/team wallets separately, ② look at the Gini coefficient together with top-concentration indicators like K%, and ③ look at a time series, not a single point in time.

Limit: the data on this page runs to November 2018. Each token's concentration may be different now. Do not use it for investment decisions.

Code 1 · Gini coefficient, decile ratio, K% Rule and a concentration model
